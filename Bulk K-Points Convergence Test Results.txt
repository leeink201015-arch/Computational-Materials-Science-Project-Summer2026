also note that E_(8, 8, 8) ​> E_(12, 12, 12) ​> E_(10, 10, 10)​, this shows that the values are oscillating closer to a certain value

This shows us that 8x8x8 is not sufficiently converged for the precision we want

Now I'm going to keep on running the convergence test with K grids (14, 14, 14) and (16, 16, 16)

Got scf_k_14x14x14.out:!    total energy              =    -254.36800680 Ry
    scf_k_16x16x16.out:!    total energy              =    -254.36796161 Ry

scf_k_14x14x14.out      total magnetization       =     2.15 Bohr mag/cell
scf_k_16x16x16.out      total magnetization       =     2.16 Bohr mag/cell

|ΔE_(12→14)| = ​0.00147153 Ry (= 1.41 meV);
|ΔE_(14→16)| = 0.00039001 Ry (= 0.61 meV),

here, the total energy converged, and note that |E_(8, 8, 8) ​− E_(16, 16, 16)​∣ ≈ 0.0009323 Ry (= 12.7 meV/atom), which is not negligible for the caluclation of Bulk Modulus

This proves the hypothesis, that the original (8x8x8) mesh had not reached the level of K-Point convergence needed for a high-precision EOS fit!

So now, we can use the 14 x 14 x 14 grid for our next EOS. Reason why 14^3 is better than 16^3 is because at 14 x 14 x 14, we're already around the ~1 meV/atom level relative to the next denser test. That's a reasonable accuracy/cost compromise for our calculation
